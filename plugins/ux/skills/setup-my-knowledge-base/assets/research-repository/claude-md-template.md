## Research Repository

A store of UX research studies and cross-study synthesis, readable by any project.

## Purpose

This folder holds three kinds of document:

- **Studies** (`Studies/`), one per study. A record card: what was asked, of whom, when, by whom, and what came back. Figures live here and nowhere else.
- **Reports** (`Reports/`), the full write-up of one study — reasoning, quotes, recommendations.
- **Topics** (`Topics/`), cross-study synthesis. A question that more than one study speaks to.

Studies and Topics are a ladder. A study says what one piece of research found. A topic says what several found together, and restates no figures of its own.

## Point-in-time rule

A study is a snapshot. It records what people said on a given date, under the conditions in place at that date. Those conditions are part of the finding and cannot be recovered later.

A finding is therefore never brought up to date. New data creates a new record — it does not edit an old one.

**Sealed once written.** No edits, no additions, no tidying, on any research report or study page. The one exception: a figure mistyped from the raw source it already cites is a correction, not an update — fix it and record what it was corrected from. New data is never a correction.

**Living, not sealed:** Topic pages in `Topics/`, since they restate no figures of their own — a new study is a new source, not a rewrite.

## PII rule

Two tiers, set by what each layer names.

| Layer | Share with | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `Studies/`, `Topics/` | Anyone | Carry no participant names. Built to travel. |
| `Reports/`, and raw source files | This organisation only | Reports name participants. Raw material may carry other identifying details too. |

- Never copy a verbatim quote into a study or topic page without checking it for names or other identifying details.
- Respondent IDs are fine. Names are not.
- A study page must carry no participant names even when its report does. Colleague attribution is fine (a "Run by" line, a source citation).

- Markdown links only, never wikilinks.
- If a claim can't be traced to a source file, mark it as needing verification.
