# AI Writing Guidelines

Rules and patterns to avoid so writing doesn't read as AI-generated.

---

## Tells to avoid

### 1. Negation framing (the #1 tell)
Avoid the structure: "[Topic] isn't just about [basic definition] — it's about [deeper definition]."
LLMs overuse this to sound profound. It's the single most prevalent AI writing pattern.
> ❌ "Design isn't just about aesthetics — it's about solving problems."

### 2. "From… to…" parallelism
Avoid using "from X to Y" to convey topic coverage. Humans use it for distance/time; AI uses it to pad scope.
> ❌ "From wireframes to final prototypes, the process has never been more streamlined."

### 3. "Whether… or…" correlation
Avoid "whether X or Y" constructions used to appear balanced or inclusive.
> ❌ "Whether you're a designer or a developer, this tool adapts to your needs."

### 4. Rule of threes + quotation marks
Avoid combining three parallel items with scare quotes around vague concepts. Common AI filler.
> ❌ "It's about "clarity," "purpose," and "impact.""

## One example house-style rule (edit or delete this section to match your own taste)

### 5. Em dashes
This isn't an AI tell — em dashes aren't a reliable sign of AI authorship on their own. It's here as an example of a personal style preference some people like to enforce: avoiding em dashes in prose, especially customer-facing copy, in favour of a comma, full stop, or parentheses. Keep it if it matches how you like to write, or delete this section if it doesn't.

- Items 1–4 don't prove AI authorship, but they create the *impression* of it.
