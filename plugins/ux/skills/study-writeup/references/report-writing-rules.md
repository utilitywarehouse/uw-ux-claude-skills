# Report-writing rules: point-in-time discipline

A report records what its study found, under the conditions in place when it ran, and it stops there. This isn't a style preference — a report read years later needs to still be a reliable record of that moment, not a mix of what was true then and what's true now.

**The test for every sentence:** could the person who ran the study have written this on the day it finished? If not, it doesn't belong in the report.

## No forward references

A report on an older study never cites a newer one, even if the newer study clearly bears on it. That connection is real and worth recording — but it belongs on a Topics page, which is allowed to grow over time, not in a sealed report that can't.

## No hindsight

Never write "four years on," "this has since been fixed," or "still open." Every one of those is a judgment about *today*, and today keeps moving — which means the sentence would need to change to stay true, and a sealed report can't change.

If something has in fact changed since the study, that update belongs in a Topics page's "what changed" section, or gets raised directly, not folded into the report.

## No knowledge-base housekeeping

Which other document has the wrong date on it, why this report is being written now rather than when the study finished, what a record card was missing before this write-up — none of that belongs in the report itself. It's process information about the knowledge base, not a finding about the study. Route it to the Research Repository's `log.md`, or raise it directly.

## "Completed" is the study's finish date

Not the date of this write-up. If a report is being written well after its study finished — closing a gap, not documenting something fresh — `Completed` still records when the study itself ended.

## The same rule applies to a card trimmed from a report

Trimming an existing card down to headlines is not automatically safe just because the report it's trimmed against is clean. Hindsight and knowledge-base-housekeeping content creep into cards the same way it creeps into reports — check the trimmed text against this same test before it's finalized, don't assume clean-in implies clean-out.
